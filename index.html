01 · TAXONOMY OF ATTRITION

Four-Tier Rarity Classification Framework

Rather than treating lexical rarity as a monolith, each archived word is classified according to the specific historical or sociolinguistic mechanism driving its recession from contemporary speech. Select any tier below to filter the archive.

TIER R1 · OBSOLESCENCE

Historical & Material Obsolescence

Words denoting pre-modern institutions, feudal architecture, agrarian weights, or traditional vessels whose physical referents have vanished from modern life (e.g., Anchal Aapees, Uari, Pandikashala).

TIER R2 · LOAN DISPLACEMENT

Colloquial Attrition & Displacement

Indigenous Dravidian or Indo-Aryan vernacular vocabulary systematically displaced in urban and generational speech by English loanwords (e.g., Theevandi → "train", Hottige → "book", Alavukol → "scale").

22 Records Filter R2 →
TIER R3 · REGISTER CONFINEMENT

High-Register & Classical Confinement

Literary Tatsama, Halegannada, and Sadhu Bhasha lexemes restricted to classical poetry, epigraphy, or scripture and absent from conversational registers (e.g., rudhira, kkhonprobha, Triyama).

TIER R4 · PURIST NEOLOGISMS

Institutional & Purist Coinages

Formal vernacular compounds coined by state language academies or lexical committees to translate modern technological concepts that remain rare in spontaneous speech (e.g., Ganakayantra, Jangamana gante).

04 · CURATED EXAMINATION

Specimen from the Archive

Inspect how individual entries combine primary lexicographical evidence with sociolinguistic documentation.

ARCHIVAL METHODOLOGY

Why document both archaic lexemes and loan-displaced vernaculars?

Linguistic attrition occurs along two distinct axes. First, when historical institutions or material crafts disappear (Tier R1), their specialized vocabulary vanishes with them. Second, in highly diglossic or globalized speech communities, indigenous words for everyday objects and spaces (Tier R2) are rapidly displaced by English loanwords in spoken conversation, surviving only in print or literary registers (Tier R3).

By pairing every entry with its verifiable 19th- or 20th-century dictionary anchor and an explicit sociolinguistic proof, Lupt Shabd distinguishes primary lexical evidence from archival metadata.

06 · COLOPHON & ATTRIBUTION

Project Principle & Contributors

An academic Digital Humanities initiative dedicated to structured, verifiable preservation of lesser-used Indian vocabulary.

Editorial & Archival Standards

Lupt Shabd was designed to model rigorous digital lexicography. Interface languages strictly mirror the archive’s holdings (Malayalam, Kannada, and Bengali, alongside English). Every record is assigned a sequential identifier (BLA-MAL-0001 to BLA-BEN-0100) and verified against primary print lexicons digitized by the University of Chicago’s Digital South Asia Library (DSAL), Sahitya Akademi, and Kerala Sahitya Akademi.

Archival photographs and manuscript plates are sourced from verified public-domain and Creative Commons holdings on Wikimedia Commons.
